Related instruments, different constructs. This page states what has actually been compared and what has not.

IGRM is a salience index

IGRM retains its established name, but it measures press salience: the share of monitored coverage matching five frozen India-relevant dictionaries. It does not measure the probability or severity of geopolitical events. It is distinct from the Caldara-Iacoviello Geopolitical Risk (GPR) family.

What has actually been tested

The published benchmark compares monthly mean IGRM percentile scores with GPRC_IND, the Caldara-Iacoviello country-specific India series. The two pipelines share no corpus, dictionary or normalisation. Across 110 shared months, the composite correlation is 0.232 in monthly changes and 0.484 in levels. Changes are the sterner test because persistent series can correlate in levels through a shared trend. The result shows limited co-movement between related measures. It is not proof that either instrument is superior.

The benchmark has moved

Iacoviello and Tong released AI-GPR in 2026. Its main index is daily and uses an LLM to score article-level risk intensity; its country decomposition is currently published monthly. IGRM has not yet been benchmarked against AI-GPR. Until that test is registered, run and published, this project will not use its daily frequency as a claimed advantage over the full GPR family.

What a superiority claim would require

IGRM does not currently claim to “beat” GPR. A defensible claim needs a frozen test, not a design comparison written after looking at the data. The next benchmark will specify the common dates, exact India series, named episodes, timing loss and missing-data rules before results are computed. A separate blind article audit needs at least one independent human coder. Failures will publish with the wins.
