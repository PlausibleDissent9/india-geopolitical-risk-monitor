# The 95-point gap at Hormuz

The daily IGRM composite closed 6 August at 48.9, down from 70.0 a week earlier. The trailing seven-day composite was 61.5. Those numbers answer different questions: the daily score ranks one day's coverage share against each channel's own trailing 730 days; the seven-day score averages a week of those daily ranks. A quieter close can still sit inside a loud week.

This release also publishes an independent audit of the NGrams splice. The available independent re-estimates would put the same daily and seven-day composites at 48.3 and 65.3. US Trade and Shipping remain at their production ratios because no new estimate exists. The level moves, especially inside the China and Pakistan channels, but the weekly reading remains above the daily one. The full sensitivity series is public, not buried.

The sharper finding is at Hormuz. In the latest IMF PortWatch week, press salience about the strait ranked at the 95.4th percentile of its 2019-present history. Measured transit activity ranked at the 0.4th. The gap was 95.0 percentile points.

This week's receipts help explain the attention: reporting covered a possible Hormuz arrangement, Iranian control of inbound traffic, tanker attacks and crude prices. They do not explain the physical count, and the index cannot adjudicate between them.

IGRM measures what got written. PortWatch measures what moved. Neither series predicts closure, prices or risk. A 95-point gap does not tell us why they disagree. It tells us where a lower headline composite is concealing a much less settled information environment.
