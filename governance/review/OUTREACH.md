# Reviewer outreach

Two independent reviewers are needed. They are different people with
different skills, and neither substitutes for the other:

- **Packet A** — code, security, release. Someone who can read Python and
  attack a trust chain: security engineer, open-source maintainer, strong
  CS postgrad.
- **Packet B** — methods and evaluation. Someone who can attack the
  statistics: econ or political-science PhD student or postdoc working on
  forecasting evaluation or conflict data. Authors of papers IGRM already
  cites are the obvious first list.

## Why the ask is shaped this way

A cold "would you review my project?" gets a polite non-answer, because it
asks for unbounded time on an unknown thing. A cold "here is one specific
question, three to five hours, and you may publish whatever you conclude"
gets replies, because the cost is knowable and the reviewer keeps their
independence.

Three things do the work:

1. **A bound.** 3–5 hours, stated up front, honoured.
2. **An adversarial framing.** They are asked to break it, not bless it.
   That is a more interesting invitation, and it is also the only kind of
   review worth having.
3. **Known weaknesses disclosed first.** Both packets list what we already
   know is wrong. It saves their hours, and it signals the review is real
   rather than decorative.

## Email template

Keep it under 150 words. The packet carries the detail; the email only has
to earn the click.

> Subject: 4 hours to try to break a published risk index?
>
> Dear Dr <name>,
>
> I'm a high-school student in India. I publish a daily India
> geopolitical-risk index at igrm.in, built from GDELT. It's public,
> versioned, and it refuses to publish a number when its sources or rights
> are unclear.
>
> I'm looking for someone to attack it rather than approve it —
> specifically <the one question from their packet, in one line>.
>
> The ask is 3–5 hours against a frozen commit, and one written verdict.
> You'd keep the right to publish your findings independently, and I'd
> publish them alongside what I did and didn't fix. Paid or pro-bono,
> whichever you prefer.
>
> The one-page scope is attached. If it's not for you, I'd be glad of a
> pointer to someone it might suit.
>
> Ishan Krishna

Attach the relevant packet. Do not attach both — sending both signals you
have not thought about which one they are.

## Notes

- Ask for a **pointer** if they decline. It costs them nothing and it is
  where most second-choice reviewers come from.
- Do not chase. One follow-up after ten days, then stop.
- Anonymous review counts. Independence is what matters, not a name.
- A reviewer who declines because the index is too weak to be worth
  reviewing has given you a finding. Record it.

## Deadlines

| Date | State |
|---|---|
| Aug 31 | Candidates identified, scope and availability recorded |
| Sep 7 | Commitments or refusals; reviewer key fingerprints prepared |
| Oct 18–21 | Review runs against the frozen candidate |
| Oct 23 | Verdicts recorded, remediations done or declined with reasons |

If nobody has committed by Sep 7, that is not a reason to loosen the
review. It is a reason to record the federation and evaluation evidence as
at risk, and to say so in the final package.
